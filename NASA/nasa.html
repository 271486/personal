Evidence notes and prototype limits
  • The supplied research notes distinguish direct laboratory measurement from wearable-derived estimates. Common wearable signals such as movement, heart rate, HRV, skin temperature, and light exposure are context data; they do not directly measure melatonin or caffeine concentration.
  • A caffeine half-life calculation is only a population-level estimate and varies between people. This prototype does not calculate caffeine clearance or recommend a dose or timing.
  • Melatonin timing such as dim-light melatonin onset requires an appropriate clinical measurement; this prototype does not infer hormone levels or recommend sleep-aid use.
  • Sleep shifting is a normal planning challenge in rotating night-work settings such as nursing, emergency response, aviation, military operations, and remote or offshore work. NIOSH notes prophylactic naps before night shifts can support alertness, while sleep inertia after waking can temporarily affect performance. These settings are useful comparison cases, not interchangeable EVA protocols.
  • For this prototype, shift sleep onset by no more than 2 hours per 24 hours. Any melatonin or stimulant use must be the minimum flight-surgeon-approved plan needed to avoid sleep inertia or excessive stimulation; no dose or timing is generated here.
  • The planning indicator is an unvalidated demonstration heuristic. It must not be used for clinical decisions, operational fitness-for-duty, or crew task assignment.
  • All generated schedules are proposals. Mission operations and flight-surgeon or fatigue-specialist instructions take priority.
