### Summary

SSRS is an offline-first student prototype for exploring personalized sleep/wake schedules around mission operations. A crew member can enter a current and target sleep onset, select a planned sleep opportunity, add a required-awake operation, and record sleep and alertness feedback. The prototype proposes a gradual next sleep period and flags overlap with entered operations.

### What the prototype demonstrates

- Local, per-crew demonstration accounts and separately stored plan/feedback.
- A conservative one-hour-per-sleep-period schedule adjustment.
- Proposed wind-down and wake times.
- An operation-conflict warning that leaves operational decisions to the mission team.
- Local feedback capture and visible limitations for the planning indicator.
- Offline operation without a cloud service.

### Safety and research boundaries

This is a student demonstration, not a medical device, validated fatigue model, or flight-qualified system. Its numeric planning indicator is an unvalidated heuristic and must not be used for fitness-for-duty or task assignment. Wearable measures such as movement, heart rate, HRV, and skin temperature are not presented as direct hormone measurements. The application does not calculate caffeine clearance or recommend caffeine or sleep-aid dosing/timing; those decisions require approved flight-surgeon and fatigue-specialist protocols. Mission operations always take priority.

### Future work

The next steps are specialist review of requirements, an approved and validated fatigue model, mission-grade local identity and data protection, human-factors testing, fault recovery, and integration testing on target hardware. Any future sensor inputs would require approved devices, provenance, units, and data-quality checks.
