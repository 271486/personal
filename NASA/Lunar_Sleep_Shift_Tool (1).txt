WHY IT MATTERS
The Moon and Mars raise the stakes
04
Communication delay
A ~2.6-second one-way delay to the Moon makes the ground 'game of telephone' awkward — and Mars is far worse.
Frequent operations
Not one EVA every few months — potentially multiple EVAs per week, each needing a re-jig of the sleep plan.
Need for autonomy
Crew must adjust their own schedules to be operationally ready without round-tripping to specialists on the ground.
An unearthly day/night
On the Moon, roughly two weeks of light and two weeks of dark remove the natural cues the body relies on.

RESEARCH
Sleep deficiency in spaceflight is pervasive
24
~6 h
average sleep per night in space — below the ~8 h scheduled (Barger et al., 2014)
~⅓–½
of nights under 6 hours of sleep on shuttle missions (Barger et al., 2014)
Widespread
hypnotic (sleep-medication) use on orbit (Barger et al., 2014)
Docking, undocking, and EVAs force 'slam shifts'
Abrupt sleep-timing changes resemble severe jet lag or rotating night-shift work — but with higher stakes (NASA Ames Fatigue Countermeasures Lab; Flynn-Evans).
Deficits degrade performance and accumulate
Six-month ISS data link sleep deficiency to degraded neurobehavioral function and elevated stress (Jones et al., 2022).
Implication: better, faster countermeasure planning is genuinely needed — exactly the gap this tool targets.

RESEARCH
Countermeasures the tool can encode
25
Light
Light is the strongest clock-setter. Timed bright/blue-enriched light advances or delays the clock; blue reduction before bed protects sleep. ISS uses tunable lighting modes for this (Wu et al., 2018).
Melatonin
Low-dose, strategically timed melatonin shifts the clock and aids sleep; guidelines endorse timed melatonin for circadian disorders (AASM, 2015).
Caffeine & alertness
Timed caffeine supports a planned alertness window without blocking the next sleep period — sequenced against the operational timeline.
Scheduling & naps
Gradual phase shifts, protected sleep windows, and strategic naps reduce misalignment — the core of analog-mission fatigue programs.

RESEARCH
Lessons from space & ground analogs
26
Phoenix Mars Lander
Mission staff entrained to a 24.65 h 'Mars day' for ~78 days using a structured light + scheduling fatigue program (Barger et al., 2012).
Mars500 (520-day sim)
Long isolation produced reduced activity and altered sleep timing/duration — a caution for deep-space confinement (Basner et al., 2013).
Concordia, Antarctica (ESA)
An ISS analog with months of darkness; blue-enriched light tested as a countermeasure to circadian delay (ESA; Najjar et al., 2014).
ESA in-orbit studies
Huginn-mission 'Circadian Light' and 'Sleep in Orbit' experiments study lighting and sleep monitoring on the ISS (ESA, 2023).
